Third Spaces
[A Field Guide]

In 1989, sociologist Ray Oldenburg gave a name to everything else. He called it the "third place": somewhere apart from home and work where people gather freely, without an agenda, and belonging is built simply by showing up again and again.

This is a running index of third spaces across New York’s five boroughs: cafés and bookstores, gardens and plazas, libraries and listening bars, makerspaces and mutual aid fridges, artist-run galleries and neighborhood pools.

Each entry includes its address, a short description of what you can do there, and the Oldenburg qualities it embodies.
